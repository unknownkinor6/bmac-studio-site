Planning Permission

Most people come to us wanting a bigger kitchen, not a lesson in planning law. It is the part that decides whether any of it happens, so it is worth handling properly. Anyone can submit an application — you can do it yourself on the Planning Portal this afternoon. The question is what happens when a case officer opens the file.

01Why it fails 02You may not need it 03What to submit 04Pre-application 05How long 06Which council

Applications fail on avoidable things

Drawings that do not match. Missing information. A scheme that was never tested against the council’s own policies. Those are process failures, not design failures, and they cost you months.

An application that pre-empts the case officer’s questions is the single biggest thing you can do to influence the outcome. bMAC Studio is led by an architect holding both ARB registration and RIBA chartered status; much of the local market is architectural designers, who cannot legally use the title at all. Why that matters.

If your application has already been refused

Some clients arrive after someone else’s application was turned down, sometimes more than once. In more than one case the previous designer had not used a pre-application enquiry, and permission was granted once that route was taken.

A refusal is not the end of the project. The decision notice states the reasons, and those reasons are the brief for what happens next.

  1. Revise and resubmitAnswer the stated reasons and go again.
  2. AppealTo the Planning Inspectorate, where the reasons are weak.
  3. Start againAccept the scheme as drawn was never going to work, and design a different one.

Bring the decision notice to the first conversation. It tells us more in two minutes than the drawings will.

You may not need permission at all

A good number of extensions and loft conversions are permitted development, meaning you can build without applying. The limits are specific, and they are easy to breach by a few hundred millimetres. The dimensional limits are on the rear extensions and loft conversions pages.

Larger single storey rear extensions can go through prior approval instead: a lighter route where the council notifies your neighbours and decides within 42 days rather than assessing the design in full.

Those rights disappear in conservation areas, on listed buildings, on flats and maisonettes, where an Article 4 direction applies, and where previous owners have already used the allowance up. We check which of those applies to your address before anything gets drawn.

Worth having anyway

Where work is permitted development, a lawful development certificate is still worth having. It is proof for your buyer’s solicitor in ten years’ time.

What a good application contains

  • Location plan and site plan to a recognised scale, correctly annotated.
  • Existing plans, elevations and sections, drawn from a survey of the house as it really is rather than from the estate agent’s floorplan.
  • Proposed plans, elevations and sections that agree with each other in every dimension.
  • A design and access statement where one is required, arguing the scheme against the policies the officer has to apply.
  • The correct application forms, ownership certificates and fee.

Half of that list is administrative. It is also where applications sit at validation for weeks before anyone looks at the design. See a drawing set that answers it.

Pre-application advice, and when it is worth the fee

Most councils will give you a written view on your scheme before you apply, for a fee. It is not binding, but on a marginal scheme it is usually the cheapest money you will spend.

It earns its fee when the scheme is bigger than the neighbours’ precedent, in a conservation area, near a listed building, or where a previous application has failed. Rarely for a modest rear extension that clearly fits policy.

How long it takes

Statutory determination periods
RouteStatutory period
Householder application8 weeks
Larger home extension, prior approval42 days · 21 days for neighbours
Full application, larger schemes13 weeks

The clock starts at validation, not at submission, which is why an incomplete application is expensive in a way that is not obvious at the time. Once granted, permission normally has to be started within three years.
