A brief, turned into 37 sheets.

A town-centre building with shops and a restaurant at street level, converted into seventeen self-contained flats on the floors above, with the commercial units kept in use. This is the set a contractor prices, a building control officer checks, and the trades build from.

What a construction sheet actually is

Not just a drawing — a controlled document. Every sheet carries the same furniture: a ruled border, a title block, a materials key running existing / demolition / new brick / new stud / new roof, a drawn scale bar, and its number in one continuous series.

The set moves in three movements: survey what is there, set out what replaces it, then specify every junction and system.

About these images

Every drawing below is from the real issued set. Anything identifying the building or the people involved has been removed: the address and street name, the client and job number, the site location and aerial sheets, and the names of neighbouring businesses. Personal contact details have been stripped too. What remains is the drawing craft and the construction content, which is what this is about.

Before any drawing, the written spec

The set opens with a sheet index and a wall of specification organised by trade — general requirements and site preparation through concrete, masonry, steel and carpentry, roofing, glazing, plastering, finishes, drainage, heating, ventilation and electrics. These clauses set the standards the drawings then apply: U-values, fire ratings, product references, tolerances, and who supplies what.

You cannot convert a building you have not measured

The set records the building as found, floor by floor and in section, so new work can be set out against real dimensions and building control can see what is kept, altered or removed.

The proposed layout

One level at a time. Walls are drawn in the key’s hatches, so it is immediately clear what is existing fabric, what is demolished, and what is new brick or new timber stud. Flats are colour-coded and scheduled with floor areas; rooms are labelled and sized.

Elevations and sections

Elevations show the building’s faces, what changes and what is kept, with finished floor levels marked. Sections cut through to show storey heights, the relationship between old and new floors, and where each construction detail applies — the dashed key tags point back to the detail sheet.

Where the set says how it is put together

Every wall, floor, roof and terrace type is drawn at 1:20 as a labelled build-up: layer by layer, product by product, with the target U-value noted. On site, the letters on this sheet are what the plans and sections refer back to.

Structure, fire and services

Three more layers run over the same plans. Structure marks which existing steel is kept, which comes out once load is transferred, and where new columns and beams carry the new layouts — deferring to the structural engineer for sizing. Fire strategy tags every flat entrance and every window onto the escape route with its rating. Services place every fitting, socket, switch, radiator and extract fan at a set mounting height.

Then every flat gets its own pair

A special facilities plan tracing kitchen and bathroom hot and cold supplies and drainage runs, including drops to and from the floor below, and a matching lighting and services plan. This is what the plumber and the electrician work from, flat by flat.

And the small things at the end

The set finishes with the external structures the conversion needs: a covered cycle stand in steel with a translucent roof, its final length set on site, and a timber-clad refuse enclosure sized for three communal bins.

→ The construction approach, in brief

External walls

  • Street wall. Existing 225 mm brickwork lined internally with 92.5 mm insulated plasterboard on treated battens. U ≤ 0.2.
  • Backyard walls. Rendered cavity, dense aerated blockwork both leaves, full-fill mineral wool, silicone through-render.
  • New cavity walls. 100 mm facing outer leaf, 100 mm full-fill insulation, aerated block inner leaf. U ≤ 0.18.

Separating construction

  • Between flats. Two independent stud frames, fireline board plus dense plank each face, 100 mm acoustic roll, clear cavity between frames.
  • Floors. T&G board on resilient cradle battens over 170×50 joists, acoustic quilt to all voids, suspended plasterboard ceiling, resilient flanking strip at the perimeter.

Roof and terraces

  • Existing pitched roof reworked around new and relocated rooflights, clay tiles matched.
  • Flat roofs: fibreglass membrane over 140 mm rigid insulation. U ≤ 0.15.
  • Terraces: hot-melt waterproofing over ~210 mm rigid insulation, porcelain pavers on adjustable pedestals.

Fire

  • FD90 self-closing fire doors to flat entrances and protected routes; fire-rated windows opening inward onto the escape route.
  • Mains interlinked smoke, heat and CO detection with battery back-up; emergency lighting to escape routes.
  • One square metre high-level openable smoke vent at the top of the stair.

Glazing

  • Argon-filled double glazing, U ≤ 1.4. Grey outside, white inside.
  • Obscure glass to bathrooms; toughened or laminated safety glass to doors and below 800 mm; trickle ventilation throughout.

Services and drainage

  • Recessed 11 W LED downlights; sockets at 450 mm; data and TV points.
  • Gas boiler and unvented cylinder where required; TRV panel radiators; electric underfloor heating to bathroom tile areas.
  • Humidistat extract fans ducted outside from every bathroom and kitchen. Soil pipes boxed in double plasterboard and wrapped.

What was redacted, and why

To keep this about the work rather than about a specific building or its owners, the following were removed from every image: the property address, postcode, street name and neighbouring house numbers; the client and project name and the job number; the site location plan, ordnance survey extract and aerial photograph, which are not shown at all; the names of neighbouring businesses on the front elevation; and the practice’s personal contact details and office suite address.
